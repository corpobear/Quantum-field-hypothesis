## 1. Purpose of this addendum

This addendum records the current state of the MCIFT cosmology scaffold after v0.16-v0.22 Big Bang / BAO / matter-power-spectrum tests.

The goal is not to claim that MCIFT has replaced Lambda-CDM. The goal is to distinguish three different claims:

```text
1. What MCIFT currently reproduces in toy/scaffold tests.
2. What MCIFT may explain mechanistically if future tests succeed.
3. What remains unproven compared with established cosmology.
```

---

## 2. Distinguishing the two standard models

Two different standard models are relevant:

```text
Standard Model of particle physics:
  describes known particles and non-gravitational interactions.
  It does not include gravity and does not identify dark matter or dark energy.

Lambda-CDM standard cosmology:
  describes the universe using general relativity, expansion, ordinary matter,
  radiation, cold dark matter, and dark energy / cosmological constant.
```

MCIFT is not a drop-in replacement for either one. It is currently best described as a speculative mechanism-seeking field scaffold that attempts to model visible/dark channel behavior and structure-scale selection.

## 7. Current strengths

MCIFT currently has the following strengths as a research scaffold:

```text
1. It provides explicit visible-manifest and dark-manifest channel terminology.
2. It contains an internal dark-channel / dark-sink mechanism rather than only naming dark matter.
3. It derives several previously tuned cosmology-scaffold controls from anchor/channel geometry.
4. It produces a recurring BAO-like scale in toy/proxy tests.
5. It identifies precisely where the model fails: raw broadband P(k) shape without growth-transfer physics.
6. It can be tested stepwise against increasingly strict observables.
```

The strongest safe claim is:

```text
MCIFT provides a candidate mechanism for dark/visible structure and BAO-like scale selection,
but it is not yet a validated replacement for Lambda-CDM.
```

---

## 8. Current weaknesses

MCIFT currently has the following weaknesses:

```text
1. The full growth-transfer function T_growth(k) is imported in v0.22, not derived from MCIFT.
2. The model does not yet calculate CMB temperature or polarization spectra.
3. The model does not yet calculate BBN light-element abundances.
4. The dark channel has not yet been tested against gravitational lensing or halo profiles.
5. The model does not yet derive late-time acceleration or dark-energy behavior.
6. A fair likelihood / parameter-count comparison against Lambda-CDM has not yet been performed.
7. Current results are toy/scaffold metrics, not observational detections.
```

```text

4. Halo / rotation behavior: dark-manifest channel must reproduce galaxy and cluster behavior.
```

## 11. Current conclusion

MCIFT is presently best described as:

```text
an internally developing, speculative mechanism for dark/visible channel splitting
and BAO-like scale selection, with promising toy/scaffold results but no full
observational validation yet.
```

The central research question after v0.22 is:

```text
Can the imported growth-transfer layer be derived from MCIFT itself?
```

If yes, MCIFT becomes a stronger candidate framework. If no, MCIFT may still remain useful as an interpretive mechanism layered on top of standard cosmological growth physics, but not as an independent replacement.
